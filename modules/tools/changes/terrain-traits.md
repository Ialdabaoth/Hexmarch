Terrain traits (Heph 2026-11-15, ATLAS_FORMAT.md). When the ruleset's movement data grades traverse (E9 does), the Atlas reads each hex's five traits (orient, traverse, hide, investigate, view) instead of travel multipliers. The traits come from the root rules module's `terrain` defaults, then the module's terrain types (module.json `atlas.terrain`), then `atlas.hex_traits` and the campaign's `hex_traits` by hex address, a finer hex over a coarser one; a road makes orient and traverse automatic, and every road is 1x. They go into the module manifest, so changing them redraws no map. Each hex of a Navigate walk passes `move` its traits and the party's speed there: a mode limited to some traverse grades counts only on them, a grade that requires a mode (E9: hard, Climb/Swim/Maneuver) puts everyone on it, and a grade with speed 0 (E9: impossible) stops the walk before that hex. How far the party sees is the ruleset's `sight-range`, asked with the hex's view grade. The Route tool, the map key and `HexmarchAtlas.travel` time a route with a dry run of `move`; the key lists each terrain's speed, its traverse grade, and all five grades in a tooltip, and each road class what it eases (E9: Forced March). The `travel` and `terrain-kind` procedures are no longer asked.
