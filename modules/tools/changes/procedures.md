Procedures, the first slice (EVENTS_FORMAT.md; Heph 2026-11-15: "Procedures lives primarily in the Atlas"). The Atlas runs procedures: the rules modules' procedures (`modules/*-core/procedures/`) are built into the page, and a module's events and its own procedures come from its `events.json`, which build.py now writes from every `events` block and `procedures/<ruleset>.json`. In Referee mode the party box has an Events section: a button for each manual trigger queues it, the queue runs, skips and reorders with a tap, and each run goes into the shown party's Events log (`parties[].log`, on that party's clock). A run that passes time moves the party's clock; other effects are listed for the referee to apply by hand. Names and macros the ruleset doesn't provide show as a warning count. Travel time and how far one sees now come from the ruleset's `travel` and `sight-range` procedures when a terrain type gives no number of its own (the maps look as before). An embedding app hears `{event: 'log', party, entry, hours}`.

Navigate (Heph 2026-11-15). With the party shown, clicking another hex opens a menu of what the ruleset offers there, the default first: "Navigate to here", with a preview of the hexes, the time, where it would stop and for what, and the rolls it makes. A second click on the same hex runs the default, and the menu's star sets it for the campaign (`defaults.hex`). Navigate walks the route hex by hex at the level shown, passing time with the ruleset's `move`, and terrain triggers along the way join the queue; e9's navigate stops at the first hex where something was queued, and Continue (or Stay) in the party box goes on. The Route tool's button is now Navigate along the stops picked by hand. The party's own hex still zooms in, as does "Zoom in" in the menu.

Movement modes (E9, Heph 2026-11-15). When the ruleset's rules give `movement` data, the party box has a Movement line for each member, and Modes… picks theirs (up to the most slots any character can have; the rule is in the tooltip). They are kept per party (`parties[].modes`). Each hex a walk crosses passes the ruleset's `move` the ground's kind (its `terrain-kind` answer) and the party's speed there: modes that only work on some ground count only there, ground that requires a mode puts everyone on it, and the party goes at its slowest member's slowest mode.
